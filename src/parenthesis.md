# Parenthesis

A **parenthesis** (parenthetical expression) is a relative clause, introduced
with {vew} (_which_), that modifies its head nonrestrictively. In other words,
it provides additional, nonessential information about the head without defining
it. If omitted, the subject of the parenthesis is implicitly the modified head.

<gloss>
gɪ  | vew   | tir     | ŋix
the | which | is good | dog
the dog (dogs are good)
</gloss>

Here, {vew tir} asserts that dogs are good (it is excellent to be a dog). We
could remove the non-restrictive modifier entirely, and the meaning of {gɪ ŋix}
would not change. In fact, we could split this sentence into two: {tir ŋix gɪ
ŋix}.

Restricted noun phrases, such as determiner phrases and pronouns, cannot be
further modified by [prepositional phrases](./prepositional-phrases.md). For
instance, {nʊ tay kwʊ} (_happy I_) is ungrammatical. Conversely, a parenthesis
can modify a restricted noun phrase.

<gloss>
vew   | tir     | gɪ  | ŋix
which | is good | the | dog
the dog, which is good
</gloss>

In this example, {vew tir} does not contribute to the identification of the dog,
nor does it describe dogs in general; it provides extra information about a
particular dog that has already been identified.
